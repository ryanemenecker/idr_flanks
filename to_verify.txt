1. make sure that the SASA is calculated only for proximal regions such that the predictor placing a distal protein region on the target does not artificially obscure a potential region for the flanking IDR to interact with due to the distal region artificially reducing local target SASA.
2. We might be over engineering the avoidance of polyW. Why not just use complexity? I think it's already a prebuilt parameter in the GOOSE SequenceOptimizer. Higher complexity should negate any 'gaming' by overrepresenting specific residues. 
3. We should consider whether 'specificity' is being overly considered. The binder will already provide most of the specificity for the target. The short flanking IDR(s) merely provide additional means by which the binder stays bound to the target. It does matter to some degree but we need to potentially recalibrate. 
4. Should we consider an option to add a short GS linker between the flanking IDR and the binder to make sure that chemistry in the flanking IDR doesn't directly impact the propensity of the binder to fold correctly?
5. We need to very explicitly avoid a situation where the flanking IDR interacts with the region of the binder that is at the interface with the target. If this happens, the flanking IDR will actually compete with the target for interaction with the binder, which is likely to actually reduce affinity overall.
